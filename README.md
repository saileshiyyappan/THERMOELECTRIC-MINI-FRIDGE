## Overview

The Thermoelectric Mini Fridge is a completed engineering project developed in September 2020 to explore semiconductor-based refrigeration using the Peltier effect.

The prototype features an approximately 45-litre insulated cooling chamber, dual TEC1 thermoelectric modules, aluminum heat sinks, cooling fans, and a 12V, 15A switched-mode power supply. The project involved thermal management, airflow arrangement, electrical integration, insulation, and mechanical fabrication to demonstrate a compact cooling system without a conventional compressor.

## Problem Statement

Conventional refrigeration systems commonly use compressor-based cooling cycles. Exploring solid-state thermoelectric cooling offers an educational approach to understanding semiconductor heat transfer, compact cooling architectures, and thermal system design.

## Project Approach

The prototype uses dual Peltier modules to transfer heat from the insulated chamber toward the external environment.

- **Thermoelectric cooling:** Dual TEC1 Peltier modules.
- **Hot-side heat dissipation:** Aluminum heat sinks and forced-air cooling.
- **Cold-side thermal management:** Heat sink arrangement for transferring heat from the chamber.
- **Insulated enclosure:** Approximately 45-litre cooling chamber.
- **Power system:** 12V, 15A switched-mode power supply.
- **System integration:** Electrical wiring, airflow management, and mechanical assembly.
